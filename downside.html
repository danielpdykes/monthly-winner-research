What would the rule do in a down month?

The original experiment buys the previous month's best S&P 500 performer every month. This page asks whether corrections, bear markets or crashes should change that, and shows the rule's output for the latest data.

Not investment advice. This is a research tool. The card below is the mechanical output of a backtested rule, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything, and it does not account for your circumstances, taxes or costs. Past results do not predict future ones.

The short answer

Nothing tested beat simply buying the winner every month. Across 17 crash episodes, 118 correction months and 65 bear months (1998–2026), no regime override survived the checks below, so the rule's output is the standard deposit in every regime.

  • Doubling down looked good after crashes (+0.85 pts a year) but nearly all of it came from one purchase, Nvidia in March 2003. Without that episode the gain turns negative.
  • Pausing buys in bear markets, with the money earning T-bill rates then invested next month, shows +3.0 pts, but it also fails when one episode is removed. It looks like timing luck, not a pattern.
  • Switching to SPY lowers the maximum drawdown by a few points in bear months but does not reliably improve returns.
  • In 1926–1997 data for a diversified "last month's top decile" portfolio, swapping to the market during bear markets and after crashes did beat random months, by about 1 point a year. That did not hold after 1998 and the proxy is not a single stock, so it is a hint, not a rule.

Caveats

  • The monthly data has no intra-month detail except for the daily SPY series used to flag crashes.
  • The stock-level results rest on a handful of purchases (Nvidia, AMD, Micron) and an incomplete candidate universe.
  • The live pick uses current S&P 500 members from a public list and Yahoo prices; verify before relying on it.
  • No taxes, costs or slippage. Cash earns the T-bill rate; fractional shares are assumed.
